Torium's operational procedures are reviewed repository runbooks tied to
machine-readable contracts. This page maps them for operators. Everything is
local-first: collectors and alerts are inactive baselines, no public snapshot
or backup provider exists, and every public-deployment step is explicitly
deferred until approved. Never paste private keys, mnemonics, signer state, or
unredacted diagnostics anywhere — record public identifiers, heights,
checksums, and redacted outcomes instead.

## Node roles and topology

`docs/operations/torium-node-role-topology-runbook.md` is the operator
runbook for the validator, sentry, full/query, public EVM RPC, and private
archive/indexer profiles defined in `chain/profiles/node-roles-v0.json`.
It covers preflight, startup, inspection, failure, and capacity procedures;
public v0 is EVM-only, and archive/explorer access can never widen the public
RPC contract. Commands in the runbook name their target role, expected
output, and stop condition.

For day-to-day localnet control (start, per-node stop/restart, status,
degraded-quorum reads), use `./chain/localnet/torium-localnet`; the
[localnet quickstart](/v0/localnet/quickstart) is the entry point.

## Observability, health, and alerts

`docs/operations/torium-chain-observability-runbook.md` documents the v0
metrics/log/alert contract (`chain/observability/observability-v0.json`) and
the bounded local health exporter. Collectors and alert rules are defined but
**inactive**; every alert in the contract names its owning procedure, and
activation with five-role scrape evidence is part of the operator deployment checklist.

## Backup, snapshots, state sync, and restore

`docs/operations/torium-chain-recovery-runbook.md` operationalizes
`chain/recovery/recovery-v0.json`: snapshot envelopes, state-sync trust
requirements (pinned dual chain IDs, genesis hash, trusted height/hash, and a
trusting period below the unbonding time), retention, and restore gates. The
contract is production-shaped but fail-closed: no public provider is
configured and **no operator recovery drill has passed yet**, so restore
procedures must be treated as unexercised until the drill evidence lands.

## Upgrades

`docs/operations/torium-chain-governance-upgrades.md` is the executable
local upgrade runbook for the governance/upgrade contract: proposal,
checksum preflight (required for the normal operator procedure), halt
height, binary swap, and forward-fix versus rollback decisions. Release
checksums, signatures, and SBOM verification for public artifacts are release-pipeline
scope and are deferred.

## Resilience and performance

`docs/operations/torium-chain-resilience-runbook.md` defines the
validator-loss, partition, restart, and corruption scenario contract
(fail-closed: no scenario may report `pass` without a runner), and
`docs/operations/torium-chain-performance-baselines.md` documents the local
performance foundation. The explorer stack runbook covers the inactive local
Blockscout inventory.

## Incident response

Classify every event as an availability failure or a suspected compromise —
uncertainty counts as suspected compromise, and availability pressure never
overrides double-sign, revocation, or evidence-preservation rules. The
[validator lifecycle page](/v0/validators) and the key-compromise runbook
carry the concrete sequences; security reporting goes through the private
advisory form described on the [security page](/v0/security).
